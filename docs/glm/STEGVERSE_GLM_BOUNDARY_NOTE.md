# StegVerse GLM Boundary Note

## Purpose

This note explains the boundary of a hypothetical StegVerse GLM declaration for a transition-admissibility layer.

The artifact is not intended to prove that StegVerse is correct. It is intended to describe what StegVerse claims, what it observes, what it assumes, what it does not claim, and where its authority and evidentiary visibility end.

## Core Claim

A StegVerse admissibility result should be read as a scoped claim:

> Given the declared StegVerse governance boundary, visible authority class, policy reference, context state, review posture, and receipt conditions at commit time, this transition is admitted or denied by the StegVerse transition-admissibility layer.

It should not be read as:

> This transition is universally admissible.

## Five-Question Boundary Model

### 1. What does the layer claim?

It claims that a proposed transition was admitted or denied within the declared StegVerse transition-governance boundary at commit time.

### 2. What does the layer observe?

It observes the transition request, authority class, policy reference, visible context state, review posture, receipt conditions, and commit-time validity result.

### 3. What does the layer assume but not independently validate?

It may assume upstream evidence truth, external legal sufficiency, downstream continuity, and completeness of later reconstruction unless those matters are explicitly inside the declared boundary and supported by retained artifacts.

### 4. What remains replayable, reviewable, or externally inspectable?

The transition receipt, authority class reference, policy reference, review posture, admissibility result, commit-time validity result, and retained replay materials may remain reviewable if preserved.

### 5. Where do authority, observation, and evidentiary visibility end?

They end at unobserved upstream evidence, external authority systems, legal interpretations outside the declared boundary, downstream consequences after commit, and evidentiary registries not explicitly referenced by the StegVerse receipt structure.

## Organization Index Boundary

The organization index answers a discovery question only:

> What governance layer declarations does StegVerse publish?

The organization index does not compose layers. It does not transfer authority. It does not certify interoperability. It does not merge semantics. It does not make an admissibility decision.

## Transition Layer Boundary

The transition-admissibility manifest answers a governance-boundary question:

> What does the transition-admissibility layer claim, what does it observe, what does it assume, what does it exclude, and where does its authority or evidentiary visibility end?

## Non-Claims

This artifact does not claim:

- universal admissibility;
- certification;
- legal sufficiency;
- automatic interoperability;
- authority inheritance;
- semantic merging;
- complete post-event reconstruction;
- correctness of every upstream source;
- GLM approval or EVIDE integration.

## Practical Value

The artifact has value even without future integration because it makes the StegVerse claim boundary independently reviewable.
