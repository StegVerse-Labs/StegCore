# Implications for Autonomous Systems

Boundary-Conditioned Autonomy and Trust generalizes across domains.

The governing question is always the same:
Is this system still legitimately allowed to act now?

## AI Agents and Tool-Using Systems

BCAT enables AI agents to:
- Operate conditionally
- Defer when context degrades
- Avoid silent overreach
- Explain refusal as success

Agents without self-revocation are not governable.

## CI/CD and Automated Infrastructure

BCAT explains why automation:
- Accumulates invisible risk
- Outlives original assumptions
- Becomes dangerous while remaining valid

Boundary-conditioned governance enables:
- Orchestrator consolidation
- Trust continuity enforcement
- Quiet-by-default automation

## Robotics and Autonomous Machinery

For physical systems, BCAT enables:
- Early degradation instead of catastrophic failure
- Context-aware disengagement
- Clear authority handoff between human and machine

Autonomy becomes infrastructure when its limits are explicit.

## Autonomous Vehicles

BCAT provides a missing layer between perception and action.

It enables vehicles to ask:
- Do I still have authority to act aggressively?
- Has context drift exceeded trust bounds?
- Should autonomy degrade before risk spikes?

This transforms autonomy from “almost deployable” to governable at scale.

## General Principle

Autonomous systems mature when:
- Action is conditional
- Authority is revocable
- Non-action is explainable
- Trust is continuous

BCAT defines that maturity boundary.
