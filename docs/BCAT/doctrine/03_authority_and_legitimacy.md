# Authority and Legitimacy

In Boundary-Conditioned Autonomy and Trust (BCAT), authority is distinct from permission.

Permission answers whether an action is allowed by policy.
Authority answers whether an action remains legitimate *now*.

## Authority as Contextual Legitimacy

Authority is the system’s legitimate right to act under current conditions.
It depends on:

- Trust continuity
- Context validity
- Custody coherence
- Temporal alignment
- Human accountability

Authority is therefore:
- Conditional
- Time-bound
- Revocable without failure

A system may retain permission while losing authority.

## Authority Decay

Authority can degrade gradually rather than failing abruptly.

Examples of authority decay include:
- Increasing context uncertainty
- Extended operation without human oversight
- Custody ambiguity
- Accumulating temporal drift

BCAT treats authority decay as a signal to:
- Constrain action
- Reduce autonomy scope
- Trigger human review
- Suspend execution

## Explainable Denial of Action

When authority is insufficient, BCAT systems must be able to explain:
- Which boundary condition weakened
- Why legitimacy no longer holds
- What conditions would restore authority

Explainable non-action is a core requirement of governable autonomy.

Authority that cannot be explained cannot be trusted.
