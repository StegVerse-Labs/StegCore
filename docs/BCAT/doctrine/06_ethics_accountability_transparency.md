# Ethics, Accountability, and Transparency

BCAT is grounded in human sovereignty.

Autonomous systems do not absolve humans of responsibility.
They must make responsibility visible.

## Ethics by Design

BCAT embeds ethics structurally rather than declaratively.

Ethical operation requires:
- Clear limits on authority
- Explicit accountability chains
- Explainable refusal to act
- Auditability of decisions and non-decisions

Ethics are enforced through governance, not intent.

## Accountability Without Intent

Autonomous systems often act without direct human intent.

BCAT addresses this by:
- Preserving custody records
- Requiring accountable ownership
- Making authority revocation traceable
- Ensuring actions can be justified post hoc

Lack of intent does not imply lack of responsibility.

## Transparency as Trust Infrastructure

Transparency is not disclosure of internals.
It is clarity of *why* actions occur or do not occur.

BCAT systems must be able to explain:
- Why authority was granted
- Why authority degraded
- Why action was refused

Trust emerges from understandable limits, not opacity.
