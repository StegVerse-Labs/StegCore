# Introduction

StegCore governs *when* autonomous and automated systems are permitted to act.

As systems increasingly operate without continuous human intent — through automation,
AI agents, and autonomous machinery — traditional models of permission and control
become insufficient. Authentication and authorization determine *who* may act, but
not *whether* an action remains legitimate as conditions change.

This documentation introduces **Boundary-Conditioned Autonomy and Trust (BCAT)**,
the governing discipline by which StegCore evaluates the ongoing legitimacy of action
over time.

BCAT asserts three foundational principles:

1. Autonomy is conditional, not default
2. Trust is a continuous state, not a one-time check
3. Non-action can be a correct and explainable outcome

StegCore does not optimize for autonomy.
It governs autonomy.

The purpose of BCAT is not to promise safety, perfection, or inevitability.
Its purpose is to ensure that autonomous action remains **inspectable, revocable,
and accountable** in ways humans can understand and trust.

This doctrine serves as the canonical foundation for all BCAT-based systems,
academic research, and operational implementations.
