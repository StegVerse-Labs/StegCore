# Boundary-Conditioned Autonomy and Trust (BCAT) — Industry Overview

Most autonomous systems fail not because they act incorrectly,
but because they act beyond the conditions under which they were trusted.

BCAT introduces a governing layer that asks:
> “Is this system still legitimately allowed to act right now?”

Rather than optimizing autonomy everywhere,
BCAT conditions autonomy on:
- context stability
- trust continuity
- accountable custody
- inspectable boundaries

This enables:
- safer automation at scale
- earlier degradation instead of catastrophic failure
- explainable non-action
- regulatory and human trust

BCAT does not slow innovation.
It makes innovation governable.

As autonomy grows more powerful,
systems that cannot refuse themselves will not be deployable.

BCAT exists to make refusal intelligent, explainable, and correct.
