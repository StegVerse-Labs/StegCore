# README / Site Excerpt: StegVerse-001 Compared to Contemporary AI Systems

StegVerse-001 is not a frontier-model competitor.

It is a governance/control-plane architecture for consequence-bearing AI transitions.

Contemporary AI systems mostly govern what models may say, access, or call. StegVerse governs whether a proposed transition may become part of system reality.

In this context, an **AI entity** means:

> A bounded, receipt-bearing, consequence-capable work-entity whose authority is externally accredited and revocable.

This framing avoids treating StegVerse-001 as a chatbot, model, or sovereign actor. Its central function is not raw cognition. Its central function is commit-time admissibility: determining whether a proposed transition may bind under current authority, policy, evidence, continuity, and review conditions.

A frontier model may generate a candidate output. An agent runtime may orchestrate steps. An enterprise platform may manage permissions. A safety case may define deployment limits.

StegVerse adds a different control question:

> Is this transition admissible now, under the declared authority and evidence state, and can that decision later be replayed, revoked, or reaccredited?

Its core primitives are:

- external accreditation;
- declared-task routing;
- authority reconstruction;
- BCAT/GCAT-style admissibility;
- receipts;
- replay lineage;
- fail-closed denial;
- revocation;
- reaccreditation.

The strongest comparison claim is therefore not that StegVerse-001 is smarter than GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Copilot, LangGraph, AutoGPT, or Waymo.

The stronger claim is:

> StegVerse relocates governance to the consequence-binding transition.

This makes StegVerse complementary to contemporary AI systems rather than merely competitive with them.
